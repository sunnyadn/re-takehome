# How the agent works

The agent is `submission/board_agent.py`, reached through `submission.agent:create_agent`. The two models, `qwen/qwen3.5-flash-02-23` and `openai/gpt-oss-120b`, work on one shared Lean file. Lean is the only judge of what stays in that file, and every statement a model writes is checked against a witness before it is allowed in.

## The file keeps the challenge's imports

The comparator compiles the challenge and the solution separately and compares the elaborated statement of each graded theorem. Under `import Mathlib` a numeral exponent on ℕ elaborates through Mathlib's default instance (`NPow.toPow`, `Monoid.npow`); under a narrower import such as `Mathlib.Data.Nat.Basic` it elaborates through core's `instPowNat`. The two are not the same term, and a proof that Lean accepts scores 0 with "Challenge and solution theorem statement do not match". Measured on `rmo_2000_6`: a 35-minute run produced a proof the REPL and `lake build` both accepted, and the comparator rejected it for exactly this. The solution therefore keeps the challenge's import lines, adds `import Mathlib` for the tactics, and when the challenge's imports are narrower than that, raises `instPowNat` above the default instance and writes every numeral exponent in a theorem statement as `(k : ℕ)`. With that header the real comparator accepts the same proof (checked with the harness image's `compare` entry point). The agent's own statement check compares against this header, and Lean's reported positions are shifted by the extra import lines, since the REPL client strips import lines before Lean sees the file. The ℤ counterpart of `instPowNat` was not needed by any sample problem and is not handled.

## The board

The file is the board. Every `sorry` in it is an open goal. One Lean check renders every placeholder as `focus skip`, so Lean prints every open goal with its hypotheses in one pass (measured: 32 ms warm, the same as a single `skip`) and names every placeholder with no goal behind it. (`skip` alone succeeds where no goal is left: measured on rmo_2000_6, a placeholder left under a step that had closed its goal was silent, took the enclosing block's goal for its own, and nine steps in a row for `⊢ 10 ≤ n` were written 34 lines above it, where nothing was open. `focus` fails there with `no goals to be solved`, and the line is dropped.) A goal is keyed by its declaration and its printed text, not by its line number, so it survives edits elsewhere in the file.

Two workers, one per model, each pick the least-tried goal that the other does not hold, ask their model for the next step, and try to apply it. An apply is one Lean check of the whole file. A step Lean rejects is not written. A step Lean accepts is still not written if it leaves the goal exactly as it was, turns a hypothesis into `True`, re-declares a name, leaves a metavariable behind, or makes the file more than 10 s slower to check, or leaves it over 25 s to check while making it slower (the judge's comparator recompiles cold on 4 cores with 180 s, and a 24 s warm file has timed out there; measured on a 4-core pod, `rmo_2000_2` spent 2229 s of 2645 s in Lean over 318 checks, with 5 timeouts and 5 container restarts). The reason goes back to the model with its next question. A model that sends the same rejected step twice loses that goal to the other model.

A reply that runs past the token cap keeps its complete top-level steps. A reply that fails as a whole is retried once as its accepted prefix, cut at the line of the first error. A reply that still fails is read for what it states: its `have` heads (above any `intro`-like line, not already on the board, not withdrawn, at most 6) are posted as facts with `sorry` at the goal and audited like any skeleton; a head Lean cannot elaborate or the audit refutes is dropped and the rest tried once more. Measured on `putnam_2018_a1` and `rmo_2001_2`: 30 and 37 replies in one run each called the divisor technique and none reached Lean, every call sitting below the first error of a long reply.

## Facts go up, not down

A model may post a fact it has not proved yet, `have h : P := by sorry`, and the fact becomes a goal of its own. Two rules keep this from becoming a tree:

* A fact posted inside the proof of another fact is moved above the outermost enclosing `have`. Facts live at the shallowest scope where they make sense, and a fact already on the board is not posted twice. Before this rule the same plan was restated 7 levels deep inside a `ring_nf` residue and 25 goals were open at once. A fact posted below an `intro`, `rcases`, `obtain`, `cases` or `by_cases` line in the bodies it sits in is not moved past that line: it may hold only under what the line named, and Lean cannot say so once the fact stands above the `have`. Measured on `rmo_2000_2`: `y³ < (x+2)³`, true under `intro hxle` (x ≤ 8), was lifted above `h1`, refuted at (9, 11) as a claim about all x, and the correct route went with it.
* A goal inside a `have` that is rejected 4 times takes the `have` down with it. Only that `have` and its body go; facts posted next to it stay, and when the file would not check without it the whole block goes back to before the decomposition. A lemma hoisted above the theorem is withdrawn the same way when its own goals are rejected 4 times. The withdrawn statement is remembered, and a `have` that states it again is refused before Lean is asked.
* When a fact is added above a goal, the goal's printed text changes (one more hypothesis), and with it the key. The new goal inherits the old one's tries, feedback and plans when it is the only goal of the same declaration and target whose hypotheses contain the old ones. Measured on `rmo_2000_6` before this rule: each lifted fact re-keyed every goal below it, tries went back to 0 and line order decided which goal was asked next, so the goal that needed the most work got 2 prompts in 28 minutes.
* A step that wraps its own tactic in a copy of the enclosing `case … =>` or `have … := by` line is read without the copy. Both models do this when the goal was printed under a `case` tag.

## Every statement a model writes is audited

Lean accepting `have h : P := by sorry` says nothing about whether `P` is true, and a false `P` costs the rest of the run: everything after it is a contradiction and nothing closes. So each statement a model authors is audited before it is written. That covers a `have` (with or without a proof body, the body is set aside), a `suffices` or `show`, a hoisted lemma, a lemma the two models share before the first step, and the answer term of a problem that asks for one.

The audit has no heuristics in it:

0. Evaluation first. When the claim's binders are ℕ and its hypotheses are decidable, one `#eval` walks the binders over 0..39 for values that satisfy every hypothesis and break the claim. A hit is confirmed by the witness file of step 3, so nothing is refused on the evaluator's word alone, and a walk that ran to the end with no hit settles the claim: no model is asked about it. Measured over 7 runs before that rule: every refutation with ℕ binders came from the walk, the auditing model's refutations were closed claims and ℤ claims, and audit calls were half of all model calls (108 of 285 on `putnam_2020_a2`, 1990 s of reply latency, one reply 482 s under the board lock). Claims the walk cannot cover (ℤ, ℝ, sets, quantifiers) go on to steps 1 to 3. Measured in the harness image: `(x+2)^3 < y^3 ↔ x ≥ 9` under `y^3 = x^3 + 8x^2 − 6x + 8` is refuted at `x = 9, y = 11` in under 4 s, and `Nat.Prime` hypotheses decide. That claim is the one that, unrefuted by the auditing model, held the `rmo_2000_2` board for the rest of its hour.
1. Lean states the claim. The claim's proof body is replaced by `extract_goal`, and Lean prints the full context as a standalone theorem, numerals typed so the text elaborates again on its own.
2. The auditing model (the one that does not narrate, see the settings below) is asked for values of the variables that satisfy every hypothesis and break the claim. Which binders are variables and which are hypotheses is decided by which ones it assigns.
3. Lean checks the witness in a file of its own: `example (vars) (w_a : a = v) … : hyp₁ ∧ … ∧ ¬ claim := by subst_vars; first | norm_num | decide | simp | …`. Only evaluating tactics are used, so the hypotheses are proved at the values, never assumed.

If that file checks, the statement is false. The step is refused with the values as feedback, and the statement joins the withdrawn list. Anything else lets the step through: the auditor found nothing, the printed text did not elaborate, or the closers could not decide it. The check is sound in one direction only. It never refuses a true statement, and it does not promise to catch every false one. A claim that only fails outside the hypotheses' satisfiable region cannot be refuted (on `y³ = x³ + 8x² − 6x + 8` every claim consistent with the single solution passes). A set-builder answer whose membership is an existential cannot be decided by the closers. An arithmetic typo the auditor does not recompute (`4072432` for `2018²`) goes through.

For an answer that is an explicit finite set, each element is also tried as a witness against the theorem's own statement before the auditor is asked, so an answer that contains a non-solution is rejected without a model call.

A goal whose target has no variables at all (no hypothesis name and no binder, only numerals) is not sent to the auditor: Lean decides it directly, and if it is false the step that produced it is refused with the goal as feedback. This is where a wrong `use` witness or a wrong case split shows up (`⊢ 0 < 1 ∧ 2000 ∣ 10 ^ 3 * 1 ^ 4 ∧ …`), and before the rule such goals stayed on the board and every step under them failed.

The audit runs once, at the single exit of the apply path, so a step that is written as its accepted prefix or as an `exact?` retry is audited too. Before that, a prefix-cut step wrote `have h1 : 2000 ∣ 4 ^ 2 * 1 ^ 5` unaudited and the closers proved two more facts and a case from the contradiction.

The auditor is always `openai/gpt-oss-120b`, including for its own steps. Measured over 12 audits, `qwen` as auditor named values that violated a hypothesis every time, at about 9 s each, and `gpt-oss` answered correctly in about 1.4 s. An auditor that has not answered in 120 s lets the step through as unverified; the call is not cancelled, since a reservation left open in the harness's ledger fails the problem, and every call the agent started is awaited before it returns. Measured on `putnam_2020_a2`: one audit reply took 482 s under the board lock, the run's only gap over 90 s.

## Techniques the file defines

Some routes the models reach and cannot finish are a fixed tactic sequence. Those are written once, as Lean tactics in `submission/techniques.py`, and placed in every solution file after the imports and the instance line, so either model can call them by name and Lean checks the expansion like any other step. The system prompt carries one line per tactic, and the file a model reads has the block replaced by one comment line (Lean gets the whole file): measured on three runs, the 4.6 KB of elaborator code sat in every step and audit prompt, and the models' calls imitated its internal forms. The first two are `pow_squeeze` and `bounded_cases` (the leaf blocks above) and `divisor_cases h`, for `h : d ∣ N` over ℕ or ℤ: an elaborator reads `N` off the hypothesis, factors a closed numeral (`2018 ^ 2`, `4072324`), keeps a variable as a prime atom (`5 * p * q` with `Nat.Prime p` in context), routes ℤ through `natAbs`, and expands to `Nat.dvd_mul`, `Nat.dvd_prime_pow` and `interval_cases`, one goal per divisor with `d` replaced. It was checked in the harness image on 9 shapes (4 s for all) and the comparator accepts a file whose preamble defines it. Measured before it on `putnam_2018_a1`: every run reached `3a − 2018 ∣ 2018²` and tried to decide the divisor set (recursion depth, heartbeats); on `rmo_2001_2` the board stopped at `m − (p + q) ∣ 5pq` with the model writing "we would need to prove this step by step". The first version took the factorisation as an argument, and in the one run with it (108 calls) the models called it 25 times, every time without the argument or with the ℤ divisor in the ℕ form, which is why the tactic now reads the hypothesis itself. It is knowledge added by hand after those measurements, and nothing turns a route a model completes into a technique for the next problem.

## Model-specific settings, and why

`qwen` narrates its reasoning into the reply when reasoning is on, so it is asked every question with reasoning off. With it on, its shared-lemma proposal was prose in 6 of 6 p09 runs, its answer-term reply was a page of derivation, and its audit replies never reached the JSON.

`gpt-oss` step replies are capped at 4000 tokens. Measured over 808 replies at 6000, the 95th percentile latency was 146 to 182 s and 65 replies passed 120 s. The harness reads a response with a 180 s timeout and treats any transport failure as spend of unknown size, which closes the problem's ledger and fails the problem whatever the proof says. 3 such failures occurred in 7213 requests. At the slow rate seen, 6000 tokens cannot finish inside 180 s and 4000 can.

## The rest

* After the cocktail, and before any model sees a goal, tactic blocks built from the goal's shape are tried, one check each (`submission/leaves.py`): a target `y = E` with a hypothesis `y ^ n = …` gets `pow_squeeze y n E` (with the substitution of a bound `c ≤ x` when there is one), a bound below with ℕ subtraction gets `Nat.exists_eq_add_of_le` then `omega`/`nlinarith`, a variable bounded above by a small numeral gets `interval_cases`, `v ^ n = numeral` gets `bounded_cases`, `d ∣ N` gets `divisor_cases`; each block runs under a heartbeat cap and finishes with `omega`, `norm_num`, `nlinarith` or `simp_all`. Measured on 3 September: `rmo_2000_2`, `rmo_2001_2` and `putnam_2018_a1` had the route on the board within minutes of the start and were lost on leaves of exactly these shapes (`hxge : x ≥ 9 ⊢ y = x + 2` under `y³ = …` was reached by 306 s in three runs and closed in none); in the harness image every one of those leaves closes with one of these blocks (6 of 6, 21 s in all).
* Before the first step a cocktail of closing tactics is tried on every goal at once, as one `first` block whose alternatives announce themselves with `trace`, so the check that closes a goal also says which tactic did it, and that tactic replaces the block.
* Problems with several graded theorems get a shared lemma first: both models are asked for the one fact both theorems need, and every distinct statement that elaborates and passes the audit goes above them.
* Answer slots are filled before any step. Numeric slots are computed with `#eval`, never guessed. Term slots take an offer from each model, and a broken offer is not used.
* An existential goal whose body is decidable (`∃ a b, …` over ℕ, or `N ∈ {n | ∃ a b, …}`, with no hypothesis named in the body) is searched before any model sees it: one `#eval` walks the binders over 0..39, and the first tuple that closes the goal as `exact ⟨…, by norm_num⟩` (or `by decide`) is written. Measured on `rmo_2000_6`: both models spent 12 minutes on `use 10, 1` and `use 2, 4` for the membership goal, each guess a model call plus an audit, and the only small witness is `a = 1, b = 10`; Lean finds it in 50 ms. Values that close nothing go into the prompt instead.
* A step that hits Lean's 120 s timeout is dropped without prefix retries. Per-check timeouts scale with the file's own check time.
* A goal rejected twice buys a plan from both models, each posted as a skeleton of `have … := by sorry` statements rather than written out. When the two plans differ, the second one opens a sibling branch of the board, and the branches race on open-goal count. Plans that were tried on a goal are remembered across a restart of its declaration and both planners are told to give a different one.
* A goal whose vocabulary is divisibility, divisors, remainders, finite sums, factorials, powers under an order, `factorization`, coprimality or `IsLeast` carries a sheet of the names the loaded Mathlib has for it, with signatures, in every step prompt. Every line was printed by `#check` in the harness image; 5 of the 78 names first written from memory did not exist there (`pow_le_pow_left`, `lt_of_pow_lt_pow_left`, `Nat.dvd_sub'`, …) and were dropped, which is the reason the sheet is checked and not remembered. Measured before it on `rmo_2000_6` (1 h): the models reached `Nat.Prime.dvd_of_dvd_pow` and `pow_dvd_iff_le_factorization` only after rounds of unknown-name rejections and a detour through factorization arithmetic, and the second half of the theorem was never reached.
* When Lean rejects a step for an unknown constant or identifier that is not one of the goal's own hypotheses, the feedback carries the 5 nearest names in the loaded environment with their types, found by a Lean `#eval` that scans `env.constants` for the name's tokens (measured: 3.5 s per name cold; top result correct on every name tried, `Nat.pow_lt_pow_of_lt_left` → `Nat.pow_lt_pow_left`). Both models write lemma names from older Mathlib, and Lean's message says only that the name is unknown. When every goal on the board has been rejected 6 times, the declaration holding the worst of them goes back to its statement and what was tried, said and planned for its goals is forgotten; there is no cap on how often, only the clock and the budget. Measured across 43 archived runs, the earlier version of this rule fired 0 times: it was reachable only when no goal could be picked, and a goal can always be picked, so a stuck board ground on unchanged until time ran out. With the rule reachable, one p09 run restarted its declaration at event 31 of 88 and closed it from the fresh start 4 minutes later.
* Before a goal's first step, Lean scans its own constants for names carrying two or more of the goal's tokens (identifiers, notation words, the type last), and the top 12 with their types join the step prompt beside the curated sheet, once per token set. Measured in the harness image: 1 to 3 s a query, `[Coprime, divisors, mul]` returns `Nat.Coprime.divisors_mul` first, `[Nat, pow, mod, add]` returns `Nat.pow_mod`, `Nat.pow_add`, `Nat.add_mod`.
* When the closer cocktail and the witness search fail on a goal, Mathlib is asked what unifies with it (`apply?` under a heartbeat cap). An `exact` answer is written with no model asked; `refine` answers go into the prompt as the names that fit. Measured in the harness image: 4 of 4 leaf goals closed by `exact` (`Nat.le_of_dvd`, `Nat.Prime.dvd_of_dvd_pow`, `Nat.sum_range_choose`, `lt_of_pow_lt_pow_left'`), about 8 s each. An earlier `exact?` on model-posted goals closed 1 of 51, which is why that one left.
* A board that has accepted nothing for 12% of the window is stalled, whatever the per-goal counts say (restarts and withdrawals do not count as progress). A stalled board takes back its innermost open `have`, on a fork when the beam has room, so the board with the stuck fact and the board without it race as siblings; when no open goal sits inside a `have` it restarts its worst declaration. Measured on p09 before the clock: 7 of 30 steps accepted, both withdrawals on one route, and the window ran out before any goal reached the per-goal restart count.
* A statement split over several lines is folded onto one line when it is written, so every reader of the board sees it.
* Both models write the Mathlib they were trained on. Measured across 9 runs, about 450 rejected steps ended in `expected command` because a tactic line ended in a comma or used `cases h with a b`, 79 were `∑ x in s` (Mathlib now spells it `∑ x ∈ s`, and on `rmo_2000_3` alone this took 65 of 149 replies), and about 90 were `7!` with no `open Nat`. Lean's message names none of these. The big-operator spelling is rewritten when a reply is read, since the rename is lexical and the term is the same; the other two are entries in a table of notes keyed on Lean's message, next to the note on truncated ℕ subtraction. After the rewrite the `∑ … in` rejections on `rmo_2000_3` went from 65 of 149 replies to 0 of 42.
* A step that makes the hypotheses more than 3 times larger without closing the goal is refused. The trigger is `rw [← Nat.mod_add_div p 9] at *`, seen in 5 runs on 3 problems: every `p` in every hypothesis became `p % 9 + 9 * (p / 9)` and no run recovered. The measure is the shared hypotheses' length ratio, counted only when one new bracketed group appears 3 or more times; 0 hits in 1450 consecutive goal pairs from passing runs.

## What it does not do

It has no measure of a step's quality beyond Lean's acceptance: a step that rewrites the goal into something larger but still valid is kept, and the other model builds on it. It re-elaborates the whole file on every check, so the median check time grows over a run. It keeps at most 3 branches of the board and ranks them by open-goal count only, which is not a measure of progress. Audits run under the board lock, so a slow auditor stalls the other worker. It bounds what one step may add to the file's check time (10 s) but not the total, and the judge's comparator recompiles the solution cold with a 180 s limit: one p09 proof that checked in 24 s warm was accepted by the agent and timed out in the comparator on a 4-core node, while a 22 s p10 proof compared in 76 s on the same node. A proof-tree representation of the board (goal and tactic nodes) was built and tested and is not wired in; the file-as-board agent described here approximates one OR level with the stall fork and the goal-level take-back, and the branches are still ranked by open-goal count. It knows one Lean technique the models do not (`divisor_cases`, above), written by hand from measured failures, and no mechanism turns a route a model completes into a technique for the next problem. Its retrieval is one step deep: `apply?` finds a lemma whose conclusion unifies with the goal, not the sequence of them a crux needs.

## Measured

Every number below is from the harness's own scoring (`run.py` at `VM_TIME_LIMIT_S=1800` or `3600`, `VM_BUDGET_USD=1.00`, one worker), run on a WSL box and a small k8s cluster, never on the sample problems' answers.

* Sample problems p01 to p10 on the final commit: 10 of 10 (p01 to p08 in 3 to 58 s, p09 in 343 s, p10 in 155 s). Most close in the first check from the closer cocktail; p06, p08 and p10 take a few steps. p01 to p08 and p10 were 9 of 9 on every gated version from v7.22 on; p09 is the sample problem whose result varied.
* p09 (`imo1964`, the one sample problem that needs a real proof) across versions: 6 of 6 runs on v7.40 (3.5 to 17 min each), 5 of 6 on each of v7.33, v7.35 and v7.39, 3 of 3 on v7.32, 1 of 1 on v7.53 (246 s), and on the final commit 2 of 3 (343 s and 918 s; the third run reached the clock with both theorems open after 508 turns). Each earlier failure had a different cause: on v7.33 the same lemma proved twice under two names with both models fanning out below it (now the dedupe and lifting rules); on v7.35 steps kept being written at goals already closed until the clock ran out; on v7.39 the `mod_add_div` unfolding applied to the goal rather than to the hypotheses, which passing runs also do and sometimes need, so it is not refused.
* `scripts/judge_check.sh` on the final commit: PASSED, p06, $0.0055.
* Six harder problems taken from the Putnam and RMO pools (`putnam_2018_a1`, `putnam_2020_a2`, `rmo_2000_2`, `rmo_2000_3`, `rmo_2000_6`, `rmo_2001_2`), run at 1 h each. `rmo_2000_3` cannot be scored by anyone: its challenge file does not build in the comparator. `rmo_2000_6` was proved by v7.56 in 35 min for $0.11, the proof was accepted by the REPL and by `lake build` and rejected by the comparator on the statement, and under the header described at the top the same proof passes the real comparator; on the final commit it scores 1 of 1 end to end, in 378 s for $0.02, the harness's own comparator run included. The other four are 0 on every version. (The second half of `rmo_2000_6` as first published asserted the least value was 20; `a = 5, b = 2` gives `a * b = 10`, and the statement in the current problem set says 10. The theorem statement itself is the one claim the audit does not test.) Each of the four reaches the mathematical crux and the board carries true facts about it, and the models do not land the Lean technique the crux needs. A 45 min run spends about $0.12 over 340 requests, so at the judged limits the $1.00 budget binds before the 8 h do.
* Transport failures (the harness marks the ledger unknown and the problem fails whatever the proof says): 3 in 7213 requests before the 4000-token cap on `gpt-oss` steps, and 4 more in about 6400 requests on 3 September, every one a `gpt-oss` call (two of them audits) answered with HTTP 200 and a body of `{"error": {"code": 504}}` or `502` and no usage block, at requests 38, 108, 411 and 599 of their runs. The two 8-hour runs of that day both ended this way, at 6653 s and 7519 s, with $0.14 and $0.22 spent. At about 5 in 10,000 per request, a run that makes 1500 requests fails this way with probability over 50%, whatever its proof says; the agent cannot retry (the ledger is already marked), so the lever is requests per solved problem, which is why claims the evaluation walk covers no longer cost an auditor call. No `qwen` request has failed this way in about 4000.
